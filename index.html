A hypercar with almost no luggage space. We worked with master craftsmen at Schedoni in Modena to create a three-piece set that married luxury leather with precision-engineered composites. It had to feel like it was designed the same day as the car. It did.

Product · Automotive
McLaren

The most aerodynamic road car ever made had almost no room for the person driving it. The luggage had to solve that without the car noticing.

The LEGO Foundation’s Copenhagen headquarters needed to embody learning through play for the people delivering it worldwide. We designed five zones around the five skills children build through play. The space reminds the team every day who they are working for. Work became the argument.

Play · Product
LEGO

The organisation that proved play changes lives had offices that told its own people to sit down and focus. The building needed to become the argument.

Nomadic living was a lifestyle choice without a design language. We gave it one. A detachable sleep-work shell, renewable energy, harvested rainwater, expandable pods. Designed to dock in communities. Built from cork and carbon-positive materials. A home that goes where you go.

Place · Residence
Rhome

A home for people who have chosen not to have one.

Titanium was Passoni’s identity. Carbon fibre was its future. We wove the two together into a frame that carried the DNA of the original without being trapped by it. The Fidia told the industry this brand was still ahead.

Product · Identity
Passoni

Passoni’s identity was inseparable from titanium. The brief was to evolve into carbon fibre without that being the last thing Passoni ever made.

A foodbank feeding 300 people a week had an empty roof. We designed a modular urban farm where the greenhouse, café and foodbank work as one system. Replicable anywhere. Built to inspire cities that haven’t asked the question yet.

Place · Community
ARC Rooftop Nursery

A community feeding 300 people a week had a roof and nothing on it. The brief was to grow food, prove a model, and make other cities want to copy it.

Two institutions asked what an autonomous vehicle should feel like from the passenger’s perspective. We mocked up semi-standing seats, family zones, wheelchair space and crash-tested every surface. Safety became the design language, not a constraint applied afterwards.

Product · Transport
TRL & Imperial College

Two of Britain’s foremost minds in transport asked what an autonomous vehicle should feel like from the inside. Nobody had asked that question seriously before. We did.

The original Defender’s bones were sound. Its future was not. We worked through every element asking what was fundamental to its character and what could be reimagined for an EV world. The result travels lightly, leaves no trace, and feels more itself than ever.

Product · Automotive
Land-e

A Defender is too iconic to retire. We asked what it would look like if a classic car received the same care as a listed building. The answer was Land-e — retrofitted, reimagined, and ready for another lifetime.

Public structures fascinate us because they are multifunctional and live inside people’s daily routines. We dug into transport hub research, took inspiration from the cathedral roof nearby, and prototyped dozens of options in VR. The result has elegance, flexibility, and a reason to be remembered.

Place · Transport
Very Light Rail

A new kind of tram needed a new kind of shelter. Not generic street furniture but a structure with character, drawn from the city around it and designed to become part of it.

Not organised around sectors — but around a quality of thinking that moves with the problem.

The commitment

Clive Hartley

Transport Design at Coventry. MA at the Royal College of Art, London.

Has worked for Mercedes-Benz, Nissan and Porsche Design, most recently as Transport Design Manager at their studio in Zell am See, Austria.

A broad curiosity in current affairs, arts and culture informs a design perspective that draws from well beyond the studio.

Dr Daniele Ceccomori

Mechanical Engineering at Politecnico di Milano. Transport Design at Art Center College of Design.

Formerly Head of Product Design at Bentley, with project credits spanning Ford, Honda, Rolls-Royce and Ducati.

A passion for classic Italian design drives his rare ability to hold technical rigour and fresh aesthetic expression in the same hand.

Nick Mannion

Where others design objects, Nick designs moments. Working closely with LEGO, he has built toy concepts, interactive experiences and playful learning activities that put the human at the centre of every decision.

His limited-edition Supermodified Mod:1 and Mod:2 robots have become cult collectables — proof that when character and form are perfectly matched, the result is something people genuinely want to keep.
